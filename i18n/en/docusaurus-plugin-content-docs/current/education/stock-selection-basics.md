# Getting Started with Stock Picking

Stock selection is not just about short-term gains and losses. You can use a fixed checklist to make sure you understand the company, price, and possible risks before deciding whether to research further.

## 1. First understand what the company does

1. What products or services does the company mainly rely on to make money?
2. Whether revenue and profits are dependent on a single customer, region or product over the long term.
3. Is the industry in which it is growing, maturing, or clearly declining?
4. Can you explain in one sentence why you bought this stock?

## 2. Check basic financial status

- **Revenue and Profit: **Look at multi-year trends, not just a single quarter.
- **Cash flow:** When profits grow, whether operating cash flow improves simultaneously.
- **Liabilities:** Focus on debt repayment pressure, interest rate changes and short-term maturities.
- **Changes in share capital: **Continuous additional issuance may dilute the equity per share.

## 3. Understand common indicators {#market-indicators}

Prices and market indicators reflect trading or financial data at a particular time. They do not establish a company's intrinsic value or future performance. Calculation methods may also vary slightly by market, data provider, and security type.

### Basic indicators

- **High | Low:** The highest and lowest traded prices so far during the current trading day.
- **Open | Previous close:** The current day's opening price and the previous trading day's closing price, commonly used as reference points for the day's price movement.
- **Turnover | Volume:** Turnover is the total value traded, while volume is the total number of shares or board lots traded. Both help indicate trading activity and liquidity.
- **Market cap:** The current share price multiplied by total shares outstanding, used to understand the company's market size.
- **P/E (TTM):** The current share price divided by earnings per share over the trailing 12 months. It may be unavailable, negative, or less meaningful when the company is loss-making or has earnings close to zero.
- **Turnover rate:** Trading volume during a period as a percentage of the relevant share count, used to gauge trading activity. The denominator can be switched between free-float shares and total shares outstanding; free-float shares are used by default.

### Advanced indicators

- **Total shares outstanding:** The total number of shares issued by the company, including both tradable and restricted shares.
- **P/E (LYR, static):** The current share price divided by earnings per share for the latest completed fiscal year. It does not reflect the latest earnings changes in the current fiscal year.
- **Free-float value:** The market value of shares available for public trading at the current price; some data providers also call this free-float market cap.
- **Price-to-book ratio (P/B):** The current share price divided by book value per share. Direct comparisons may be inappropriate across industries with different asset structures.
- **52-week high | 52-week low:** The highest and lowest prices recorded during the past 52 weeks, showing where the current price sits within that range.
- **Free-float shares:** The number of shares currently available for public trading.
- **Amplitude:** The difference between the highest and lowest prices during a period as a percentage of a reference price, used to indicate the size of price fluctuations.
- **Volume ratio:** The current average trading volume per minute divided by the average volume per minute for the corresponding periods over several previous trading days. A value above 1 generally indicates more active trading than usual.
- **Dividend:** The cash dividend paid per share for a specified period.
- **Dividend yield:** Annual dividend per share as a percentage of the current share price. A historical dividend yield does not guarantee future distributions.
- **Board lot size:** The number of shares in one standard board lot for the relevant market.
- **All-time high | All-time low:** The highest and lowest prices in the available data history. These values may depend on price-adjustment methods and the data start date.
- **Average:** The arithmetic mean of the specified indicator over the selected period. Its exact meaning depends on the indicator and time range shown on the current page.
- **Share conversion ratio:** The number of underlying ordinary shares represented by one ADR, or the agreed conversion ratio between ordinary shares and depositary receipts.
- **Free-float market cap:** The current share price multiplied by free-float shares: `Free-float market cap = current share price × free-float shares`.

### Core indicators

- **ADR equivalent price:** The ADR price converted into a comparable price for the underlying ordinary shares using the exchange rate and share conversion ratio: `ADR equivalent price = current ADR price × exchange rate ÷ share conversion ratio`. The quoted currencies, trading hours, and fees should also be considered.
- **Relative to the Hong Kong share:** The premium or discount of the ADR equivalent price to the current price of the corresponding Hong Kong-listed share, usually shown as a percentage.
- **Currently viewed stock:** Identifies the security to which the core indicators apply, preventing confusion when comparing an ADR, Hong Kong-listed shares, or another listing class.
- **Timestamp:** Shows when the quote or conversion result was last updated. Cross-market comparisons should account for differences in trading sessions and quote times.

## 4. Write down the risk boundary before buying

1. Whether the planned investment amount will affect daily life and emergency funds.
2. What changes in facts would overturn the original judgment.
3. Whether it can withstand significant price fluctuations or long-term stasis.
4. Whether there is excessive concentration in the same stock, industry or market.
